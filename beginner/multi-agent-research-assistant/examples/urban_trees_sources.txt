Urban trees can provide shade and cool nearby surfaces. Their leaves also release water vapor through transpiration, which can cool the surrounding air. The size of the effect varies with climate, tree species, canopy size, water availability, and urban form.

Trees can improve stormwater management by intercepting rainfall and helping soil absorb water. They can also provide habitat and improve the experience of streets and public spaces.

Urban forestry programs require ongoing maintenance, watering during establishment, suitable planting space, and attention to infrastructure conflicts. Benefits are not distributed automatically: planting plans should prioritize neighborhoods with low canopy cover and high heat exposure, while involving residents in decisions.
