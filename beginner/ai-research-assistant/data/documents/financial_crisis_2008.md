# The 2008 Financial Crisis
The 2008 financial crisis was triggered by a collapse in the United States housing market and the widespread failure of mortgage-backed securities.
Subprime mortgages were given to borrowers with weak credit, then bundled into complex financial products that were rated as low-risk. When housing prices fell, defaults rose sharply.
Major institutions including Lehman Brothers collapsed, credit markets froze, and governments intervened with bank bailouts and stimulus measures.
The crisis led to a global recession, rising unemployment, and new financial regulations such as the Dodd-Frank Act in the United States.
