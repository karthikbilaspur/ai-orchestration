# Intermittent Fasting
Intermittent fasting is an eating pattern that cycles between periods of fasting and eating. Common methods include the 16:8 method, the 5:2 diet, and alternate-day fasting.
Studies suggest intermittent fasting may help with weight loss by reducing calorie intake and improving metabolic health. It may improve insulin sensitivity and reduce inflammation in some adults.
Risks include hunger, fatigue, headaches, and difficulty concentrating, especially in the first weeks. It is not recommended for pregnant people, people with eating disorders, or people with certain medical conditions without medical supervision.
Evidence is mixed for long-term sustainability. Some people regain weight after stopping, and benefits depend on overall diet quality and adherence.
